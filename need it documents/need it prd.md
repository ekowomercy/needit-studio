# **NeedIt Studio**

## **Product Requirements Document (PRD)**

**Product:** NeedIt Studio  
**Product type:** AI-powered need-to-solution marketplace  
**Primary experience:** “Tell us what you need.”  
**Initial market:** Nigeria, with potential for global expansion

---

# **1\. Product Vision**

NeedIt Studio is a platform where people can find products, services, and people to solve everyday needs without having to search through multiple apps.

Instead of asking users to know **where** to search, NeedIt asks:

> **“What do you need?”**

A user can type:

* “I need a plumber tomorrow.”  
* “I need a used washing machine.”  
* “I need someone to design a flyer.”  
* “I need a tutor for my child.”  
* “I need someone to clean my house.”  
* “I need a photographer for Saturday.”  
* “I need a laptop under ₦300,000.”

NeedIt understands the request and connects the user with relevant **people, products, or services**.

---

# **2\. Problem**

Today, finding something often requires knowing which platform to use.

Someone looking for a plumber might search Google or social media.

Someone looking for a used washing machine might check Facebook Marketplace or classified websites.

Someone looking for a designer might search Instagram.

Someone looking for a tutor might ask friends or search different platforms.

This creates:

* Too much searching  
* Too many platforms  
* Too many irrelevant results  
* Difficulty comparing options  
* Difficulty knowing who is trustworthy  
* Time wasted contacting multiple people

NeedIt Studio brings these needs into one experience.

---

# **3\. Core Product Promise**

### **“Tell NeedIt what you need. We'll help you find it.”**

The user does not have to understand the marketplace.

They don't need to know:

* Which category to choose  
* Which type of provider they need  
* Which platform to search  
* What keywords to use

They simply explain what they want naturally.

# **6\. Need Understanding**

When a user submits a request, NeedIt identifies what the person is actually looking for.

For example:

### **User:**

> “I need someone to design a flyer for my birthday.”

NeedIt identifies:

**Need:** Flyer design  
**Solution type:** Service  
**Provider:** Graphic designer

---

### **User:**

> “I need a used washing machine under ₦150k.”

NeedIt identifies:

**Need:** Washing machine  
**Solution type:** Product  
**Condition:** Used  
**Budget:** ₦150,000

---

### **User:**

> “I need someone to teach my 10-year-old mathematics.”

NeedIt identifies:

**Need:** Mathematics tutoring  
**Solution type:** Person/service  
**Customer:** Child  
**Subject:** Mathematics

The user does not have to manually select these categories.

# **9\. Matching**

NeedIt should prioritize solutions based on what matters to the user's request.

Important matching factors include:

* Relevance  
* Location  
* Price  
* Availability  
* Ratings/reviews  
* Quality of previous work  
* Seller/provider reliability  
* User preferences  
* Delivery/service area

The user should feel:

> **“NeedIt understood what I was asking for.”**

rather than:

> “I searched for something and got hundreds of random results.”

---

The user can continue the conversation rather than starting a new search.

# **12\. Trust & Safety**

Trust is one of the biggest challenges for a marketplace.

NeedIt should therefore include:

### **Verification**

Where possible, providers and sellers can verify their identity or business.

### **Ratings and reviews**

Customers can review completed transactions.

### **Report button**

Users can report:

* Fraud  
* Fake products  
* Misleading listings  
* Poor conduct  
* Suspicious accounts

### **Transaction history**

Users should be able to see their previous requests and transactions.

### **Provider reliability**

NeedIt can eventually identify providers who:

* Frequently respond  
* Complete jobs  
* Receive positive reviews  
* Cancel frequently  
* Receive complaints

The goal is to help users make informed choices.

# **18\. NeedIt Request Matching**

A major feature should be **NeedIt Requests**.

Instead of only searching existing listings, users can submit a request.

Example:

> **I need a used washing machine under ₦150,000 in my area.**

NeedIt can identify relevant sellers and available listings.

If there isn't an immediate match, the request can remain active so suitable providers can respond when available.

This makes NeedIt more than a search engine.

It becomes a **demand marketplace**.

# **24\. Key Differentiator**

NeedIt Studio should **not feel like another marketplace with a search bar.**

Its defining feature is:

## **Intent-based discovery**

Traditional marketplace:

> Category → Subcategory → Filters → Search → Results

NeedIt:

> **Need → Understanding → Matching → Solution**

That's the experience that should define the brand.

---

# **27\. Business Model**

NeedIt can eventually make money through several channels.

### **Transaction commission**

NeedIt takes a percentage from completed transactions.

### **Provider subscriptions**

Providers can pay for enhanced visibility or additional business features.

### **Promoted listings**

Businesses can pay to appear more prominently when relevant.

### **Lead fees**

Providers can pay for qualified customer leads in selected categories.

### **Business accounts**

Companies can pay for additional tools and visibility.

The business model should not compromise the relevance of results.

---

# **28\. Success Metrics**

The most important question is not simply:

> “How many people downloaded NeedIt?”

The key questions are:

* How many users submit a need?  
* How quickly do they find a relevant solution?  
* How many requests result in a contact?  
* How many contacts become completed transactions?  
* How often do users return?  
* How many providers receive genuine customers?  
* How satisfied are users with their matches?

The ultimate measure is:

> **Did NeedIt successfully solve the user's need?**

> “I'm moving into a new apartment next week. I need a cleaner, a plumber to fix a leaking tap, a used fridge, and someone to move my furniture.”

Instead of performing four separate searches, NeedIt could organize everything into:

### **Your Moving Needs**

**Cleaning**  
→ Available cleaners

**Plumbing**  
→ Available plumbers

**Fridge**  
→ Matching used fridges

**Moving**  
→ Available movers

The user manages everything from one place.

# **31\. Product Principle**

Every major product decision should pass this test:

> **Does this make it easier for someone to get what they need?**

If a feature makes users search more, fill unnecessary forms, navigate complicated categories, or repeat information they have already given NeedIt, it should be reconsidered.

The product's greatest advantage should remain its simplicity:

# **I need something → I tell NeedIt → NeedIt finds a solution.**
